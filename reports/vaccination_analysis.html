Vaccination rates across the five feature groups

Descriptive analysis of 26,707 training respondents. Overall uptake: H1N1 21.2%; seasonal flu 46.6%. Test data have no observed outcomes and are excluded. Associations do not establish causation or independent effects.

Charts use observed values only, excluding missing values separately for each feature. Bars show vaccination percentages; counts and Wilson 95% intervals are in vaccination_rates_by_category.csv. Cramér’s V measures the strength of each individual feature’s unadjusted association (0 to 1); it is not a joint group effect. Groups have different numbers of features, so their maxima should not be treated as a group ranking.

Summary of the five hypotheses

GroupObserved association with vaccination
DemographicsAge is particularly associated with seasonal vaccination: 28.5% for ages 18–34 versus 67.4% for ages 65+. Demographic associations with H1N1 are smaller; education has the largest association within this group.
Socioeconomic circumstancesAmong respondents reporting insurance status, insured people have higher uptake: H1N1 31.8% versus 14.7%, seasonal 53.8% versus 22.9%. Insurance is missing for 46%, limiting generalisation. Seasonal uptake is 49.7% above $75,000 income versus 36.3% below poverty.
Doctor recommendationsLarge differences for the corresponding vaccine: H1N1 53.2% with a recommendation versus 13.6% without; seasonal 73.8% versus 34.6%.
Health literacy and behaviourVaccine effectiveness opinions and perceived disease risk have the strongest associations within this group. H1N1 uptake is 51.1% at risk rating 5 versus 8.8% at rating 1; seasonal uptake is 74.5% versus 20.9% for its corresponding risk ratings. H1N1 uptake is 27.8% at knowledge score 2 versus 14.4% at score 0. Individual hygiene behaviours generally have smaller associations than these opinion measures.
Medical conditionsRespondents reporting a chronic condition have higher uptake: H1N1 27.5% versus 18.9%, seasonal 60.4% versus 41.5%.

All five groups contain features associated with uptake in this sample. These descriptive patterns support further investigation of the hypotheses, but do not confirm causal influence, establish an independent contribution for each group, or imply that every feature matters. Age, access to care and other factors can overlap across groups. Missing responses may be systematic; this summary uses observed responses only. No adjusted model or formal hypothesis significance tests were performed.

Missing values and interpretation

This analysis uses original observed training responses only. Missing answers are excluded separately for each feature or pair. No imputation is performed and the team cleaning pipeline is unchanged. Industry and occupation may be not applicable for non-employed respondents. Undefined pairwise associations are shown as N/A when a variable has only one observed category in the paired subset. These are unadjusted associations, not causal or independent effects.
